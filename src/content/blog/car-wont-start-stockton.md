You turn the key or press the button and nothing useful happens. It happens in driveways in Lincoln Village, in parking lots outside the malls on Pacific Avenue, and at gas stations on Hammer Lane every day. Most no-start problems fall into a few patterns, and the sound your car makes tells you which one you have.

Work through this checklist before you call anyone: several of the most common causes take about 30 seconds to fix yourself, with no tools.

![Flowchart for a car that won't start: rapid clicking points to the battery and a jump start, no sound points to the key fob, gear selector or battery terminals, and cranking without starting points to fuel or ignition and a tow to a mechanic](/img/blog/wont-start-flow.svg)

## If you hear rapid clicking

A fast **click-click-click** with dim or flickering dashboard lights is the classic sign of a **weak or dead battery**. The starter is trying, but there is not enough power to turn the engine.

- **Check the battery terminals.** A loose clamp or white, crusty corrosion on the posts can block power. If you can safely tighten a loose clamp, try again.
- **Try a jump start.** If you have cables and a second car, that is a five-minute job. If you do not, a [roadside jump start](/services/jump-start/) comes to you with a commercial jump pack.

Valley heat is the hidden reason so many batteries fail here. Long runs of 100-degree days wear out the inside of a battery all summer, and it often gives out on the first cold morning in November. If yours is more than about four years old and cranking has felt slow, get it tested. Most auto parts stores on March Lane and Pacific Avenue test batteries for free.

## If you hear nothing at all

No click, no crank, but the dashboard lights come on:

- **Check the gear selector.** Most cars will not start unless they are fully in **Park** (or Neutral). Move the shifter to Park firmly and try again.
- **Press the brake firmly.** Push-button cars need the brake pedal pressed down to start.
- **Check the key fob.** A weak fob battery can stop a push-button car from detecting the key. Hold the fob right against the start button and try again. Many cars have a backup spot for exactly this.
- **Look for a security light.** A flashing key or lock symbol can mean the car does not recognize the key. Try your spare key.

If nothing lights up at all when you open the door, the battery is completely dead or disconnected. Start with the terminals, then a jump.

## If the engine cranks but won't fire

The engine turns over at normal speed, *rr-rr-rr*, but never catches. That means the battery and starter are working. The problem is **fuel, spark or a sensor**.

- **Check the fuel gauge.** It sounds obvious, but a faulty gauge or a sender stuck on a quarter tank fools a lot of people. If you might be out, [fuel delivery](/services/fuel-delivery/) costs far less than a tow.
- **Do not keep cranking.** Long cranking drains the battery and can overheat the starter. Try for a few seconds, wait 30 seconds, and try again two or three times at most.
- **Note any warning lights or smells.** A strong smell of fuel suggests flooding. A flashing check-engine light suggests an ignition or sensor fault.

A car that cranks but won't start usually needs a mechanic with a diagnostic scanner to find the fault, and that means a [tow](/services/emergency-towing/) to the shop.

## If the car starts, then dies

If it starts with a jump but dies again within a few minutes, or the battery warning light stays on while you drive, the **alternator** is probably not charging. A new battery will not fix that. Drive straight to a shop if it is close, or have it towed so you are not stranded again halfway there.
