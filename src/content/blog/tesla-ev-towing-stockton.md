Electric cars are now everywhere around Stockton, from Spanos Park and Brookside driveways to the chargers near the Weberstown and Sherwood malls. When one needs a tow, it cannot be treated like a gas car. Here is how to get an EV moved without damaging it.

## Why an EV needs a flatbed

In most electric cars the drive motors are connected directly to the wheels. Even with the car off, turning the wheels spins the motors, and a spinning motor generates electricity. A traditional wheel-lift lifts one axle and lets the other roll on the road. On an EV, if the rolling wheels are drive wheels, that can **overheat the motor and damage the inverter and other electronics**.

That is why Tesla and most other EV makers say the car should be transported on a **flatbed**, with all four wheels on the deck. For all-wheel-drive EVs, which describes many Teslas, there is no safe way to tow with any wheels on the ground.

![Right and wrong ways to tow an electric car: on a flatbed with all four wheels on the deck marked correct, and on a wheel-lift with the rear wheels turning on the road marked wrong](/img/blog/ev-tow-right-wrong.svg)

## Put the car in transport mode first

To get an EV onto a flatbed, it needs to roll freely while the operator winches it up. Most EVs hold the parking brake on and resist rolling unless you tell them otherwise.

**On a Tesla,** this is called **Transport Mode**. On most models it is under **Controls, then Service, then Towing**. It keeps the parking brake released while the car is loaded. The screen must be on and the car awake. Transport Mode usually turns off if the car loses power or you leave with the key, so set it just before the truck arrives.

**On other EVs,** look in the owner's manual or the car's settings for "tow mode," "neutral hold" or similar. If you are not sure, tell the dispatcher the make and model and the operator will handle it.

## If the car is completely dead

A totally dead EV, where nothing lights up when you open the door, cannot enter transport mode on its own. That usually means the small **12-volt battery** has failed, not the main battery. You have two options:

- **Wake it with 12-volt power.** An operator can sometimes connect a jump pack to the car's 12-volt terminals so the screen comes on long enough to enable transport mode. Where the terminals are depends on the model, so check the manual first.
- **Load it on dollies or skates.** If the car will not wake up, the operator places wheel dollies or skates under the locked wheels and winches it aboard without the wheels turning.

Either way, mention it on the phone so the right equipment comes the first time.

## Before the truck arrives

- **Set transport mode** if the screen works.
- **Take your key card or phone key with you**, and any valuables.
- **Note where you want it taken.** A Tesla service center, a body shop that works on EVs, or your home. Some EV service centers are in Sacramento or the East Bay, which makes it a [long-distance tow](/services/long-distance-towing/), so ask for that quote.
- **Tell the dispatcher if the car was in a collision.** Damaged EV batteries need careful handling, and the operator needs to know.

## Running out of charge is common here too

The most common EV tow in the valley is not a breakdown at all. It is a car that ran low on the way back over the Altamont, or down I-5 toward Westley and the long gaps between fast chargers. Cold winter mornings and hot summer afternoons both cut range. If you are getting low, a short detour to a charger is almost always cheaper than a tow.
